The problem

The same additive, two different answers

Reported methane improvements from biochar and carbon materials range from a few percent to almost double. The published number carries the additive, the inoculum, the substrate, the S/I ratio, the reactor and the temperature, all at once.

Most papers report only the cumulative endpoint. One number per treatment cannot tell you whether the additive made digestion faster or made it produce more.
The question

Can a model trained across heterogeneous studies attribute a methane improvement to the material itself, once inoculum and operating conditions are accounted for?

If the answer is yes, the same framework predicts which material and dose is worth testing before anyone runs a batch. If the answer is no, that result is worth publishing too, because it tells the field what its datasets can and cannot support.

Modelling framework

Four stages, kinetics before regression

STAGE 01

Curated dataset

Time-resolved methane curves from published and shared experiments, stored with their substrate, inoculum and reactor metadata intact.

STAGE 02

Kinetic fitting

Modified Gompertz and first-order fits per experiment, giving lag phase λ, maximum rate Rₘ and ultimate yield P for every replicate.

STAGE 03

Cross-study regression

Random forest and gradient boosting on the kinetic parameters, with material descriptors, inoculum descriptors and operating conditions as separate feature blocks.

STAGE 04

Attribution and validation

SHAP values to see which block carries the effect, then held-out validation on studies never used in training.

The target variables are kinetic parameters, not raw yields. That is the reason time series matter more to this project than endpoint tables.
Why time-resolved data

One endpoint gives one number. A curve gives three.

λlag phase
Rₘmaximum rate
Pultimate yield
  • Conductive additives are expected to act mainly on λ and Rₘ, through interspecies electron transfer, not necessarily on P.
  • Endpoint-only data collapses all three into one value, so the mechanism becomes invisible and the model can only learn correlations.
  • Replicate-level curves also give an honest error estimate, which decides whether a reported improvement is real or within scatter.
Evidence already assembled

The repository separates working evidence from open hypotheses

  • Kozłowski et al. (2025): 12 reactor-level food-waste trajectories across four carbon-material treatments.
  • Zhang et al. (2022): author-shared methane, pH and VFA summaries, analysed without reconstructing the lost triplicates.
  • Software checks: automated tests, kinetic baselines, batch holdouts and reproducible reference outputs.
  • Supported: an auditable pipeline and dataset-specific kinetic comparisons.
  • Not yet supported: a universal material mechanism or independently validated dose–temperature response.
  • Validation gate: new reactor-level, multi-dose data with intact controls and metadata.
The project is designed so that a negative external result changes the model rather than being hidden by it.
Hohenheim discussion

Where your experimental judgement matters

  • Protocol comparability. Which inoculum-handling and BMP-method details must be modelled rather than treated as background noise?
  • Inoculum variability. Which routinely recorded descriptors are realistic candidates for explaining between-experiment effects?
  • Material experiments. Which hydrochar, HTC-liquor, digestate or carbon-additive studies retain usable raw time series and controls?
  • Failure criteria. What result would convince you that cross-study attribution is not scientifically defensible?
Question for you: which of your studies do you consider the most reliable source of raw kinetic data, and which ones would you steer me away from?
